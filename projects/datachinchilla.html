The problem

Good free material to learn data and tech exists, but it is scattered, and the hard part is the order: what comes first, what can be skipped, and what a given role really asks for. People who already know half of it still get sent through the whole catalog.

What was built

  • Ordered paths for Snowflake, dbt, Airflow, Big Data, SQL and Python, machine learning, MLOps, LLMs and agents, cloud certifications, CS50 and more. Long courses were split along the structure their authors publish, so a step fits in a week.
  • A path builder where levels from any path are picked, reordered and saved as a personal path, with its own map, progress and a link to share it.
  • A weekly planner that turns a path into this week's blocks of study.
  • Interview practice with the Blind 75 and SQL interview queries, tracking what was solved.
  • Progress that works without an account. Everything is saved in the browser; signing in only syncs it to another computer.

A path from your CV

A CV is pasted or uploaded, a target role is chosen, and what is left is the short list of what is missing, instead of the whole catalog.

A CV is pasted or a PDF is droppedthe PDF is read in the browser with pdf.js
The text is matched against a vocabulary of topicseach topic has its own signals: tools, techniques, synonyms
The level of each topic is estimatedby counting distinct signals, and skipping the ones written in the negative
The target role is compared against iteach role asks for topics with a weight

Your CV stays on your device

The CV is never uploaded. It enters as text, is matched in the browser and produces a list of steps; nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is stored unless the person saves the path.

Reading what a CV does not say

The first version looked for a signal and, if it was there, marked the topic as known. It did not look at what surrounded it: no experience with Spark counted as Spark. Negative mentions are now detected before a topic is counted. And the level is estimated by counting distinct signals of the same topic: a CV that says SQL says less than one that says SQL, Postgres, BigQuery and window functions.

Keeping hundreds of links alive

Hundreds of external links break over time. So the checks are code, not a to-do:

  • A link checker runs over every link of the site. In its first run, 434 links were checked and 126 that had moved were pointed to their new place.
  • A coverage report measures, for each role, how much of what it asks for has material behind it. A role that needs a topic with no steps cannot be completed, however many paths exist. Today every role is at 100%.
  • The catalog the pages use is generated by reading the pages themselves, so there is a single source of truth and no second list to keep in sync.

Built and run by one person: content curation, product, front end and the scripts that keep the catalog in shape.
